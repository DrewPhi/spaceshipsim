# The main point of the game

## The promise

Explore a universe that becomes deeper the more you investigate it.

The player captains or helps operate a ship, encounters strange phenomena and
alien civilizations, and follows their curiosity into an unfolding story.
AI generates new storylines, cultures, people, histories, discoveries, and
consequences in response to what the crew actually does and asks. Those details
become part of a persistent, consistent universe that can be revisited and explored
further.

The reward is discovering something fascinating, understanding something that was
mysterious, forming a relationship, or changing what happens next. Operating the
ship supplies the means, constraints, and teamwork that make those discoveries
earned and consequential.

**The question every feature must answer: what does this let the player discover,
understand, decide, or change?**

## What the player should experience

- **Curiosity:** something specific and unusual invites investigation.
- **Discovery:** an action reveals meaningful information that was unavailable before.
- **Interpretation:** evidence connects to a culture, history, physical process,
  person, conflict, or larger mystery.
- **Agency:** the player chooses what to ask, investigate, believe, disclose,
  attempt, protect, or leave alone.
- **Consequences:** people and situations respond, opening or closing possibilities.
- **Continuity:** discoveries remain true, relationships remember, and returning
  later reveals developments that follow from what happened.

A session should leave the player with a story they can tell: “We discovered this,
thought it meant that, tried something, and then this happened.”

## The discovery loop

1. Encounter an intriguing signal, vessel, environment, object, or discrepancy.
2. Choose a question and use the ship or conversation to investigate it.
3. Receive a concrete discovery: a translated message, observable behavior,
   meaningful measurement, testimony, artifact, record, or spatial relationship.
4. Connect that discovery to what is already known. New questions become apparent.
5. Decide which question or relationship to pursue and how to act on it.
6. Let the world respond with a causally connected development.
7. Preserve the discovery and consequences; continue locally, follow a lead,
   revisit an earlier contact, or travel somewhere else.

There must be satisfying discoveries along the way. The player should not have to
finish an entire investigation before anything interesting happens.

## AI is central to the experience

AI should generate and develop substantive content during play: alien cultures,
individual perspectives, historical events, strange phenomena, documents,
conversations, competing explanations, and connected story developments.

Player questions and actions determine which details need to exist next. A question
about a greeting might uncover a naming custom; a follow-up about that custom might
reveal family structure, a historical catastrophe, or a disagreement between
generations. Those discoveries should affect later conversations and choices.

The story can branch, deepen, intersect with another story, or be left unresolved.
It must support free-form questions and unexpected approaches, rather than require
a fixed sequence of dialogue choices or task buttons.

The simulation still validates physical actions, observations, permissions, and
resources. AI proposals become persistent canon only after validation. This boundary
supports the creative experience: the player can trust that a discovery will matter
and that the universe will remember it.

An offline fallback keeps the ship operable and supports testing. Passing a fallback
playtest does not demonstrate that the AI-driven discovery experience is successful.

## Potentially endless depth, generated when explored

“Infinite lore” means there is no fixed authored endpoint to discovery. It does not
mean generating an infinite encyclopedia before play or producing endless text on
every button press.

Generate detail where curiosity leads. Preserve it once established. Retrieve the
relevant history when a person, place, or question comes up again. Keep responses
readable and let the player ask for more depth.

Alien civilizations should have connected dimensions that can be explored:

- Biology, perception, habitat, and how these shape communication and technology.
- Daily life, work, relationships, family structures, art, humor, and aspirations.
- Values, rituals, institutions, taboos, law, and disagreements about them.
- Languages, names, symbols, records, and culturally dependent meanings.
- Histories, migrations, discoveries, losses, myths, and disputed memories.
- Internal factions and individual differences; one speaker never represents every
  member of a civilization.
- Relationships with other peoples and phenomena, including misunderstandings and
  consequences of earlier contact.

These details must connect to encounters and choices. Knowing a custom might change
how the crew interprets a transmission or negotiates permission to investigate.
Learning history might reveal why two honest people give incompatible accounts.

## Instruments reveal the story

Each instrument needs a clear purpose and a meaningful output. Measurements should
support discoveries and decisions, with units, uncertainty, and explanations of
what the evidence does and does not establish.

- Decoding a signal reveals an actual communication or interpretable structure,
  giving the crew something to respond to or investigate.
- PCA or other processing helps when contamination obscures useful information.
  Clean signals should not require obligatory processing rituals.
- A composition scan can reveal a material relationship that connects an artifact
  to another site or contradicts an account of its origin.
- A vessel scan can reveal capabilities, damage, or behavior that affects how the
  crew approaches a conversation or offers assistance.
- Direction finding and navigation lead somewhere meaningful: a source, site,
  meeting, phenomenon, or a new observation that changes the interpretation.
- Engineering and Tactical create consequential choices about how to investigate,
  survive, protect others, or take a risk.

A routine or negative result can be useful. Not every scan should trigger drama.
But repeated actions must not amount to progress bars followed by “complete.”
If a result adds no knowledge, eliminates no possibility, and changes no decision,
its role in the game needs reconsideration.

## A consistent world, with fallible accounts

- Established facts, identities, chronology, physical rules, and prior observations
  constrain new generation.
- Separate objective truth, measurements, testimony, cultural beliefs, rumors,
  translations, and the player's hypotheses.
- NPCs know only what they could know, and can be mistaken, evasive, or dishonest.
- New revelations can reinterpret old evidence, but cannot silently rewrite it.
- Generate enough underlying cause early to make investigation coherent; leave
  unrelated detail open until needed.
- The player can propose an explanation without making it true. The world should
  sometimes surprise or contradict them with supported evidence.
- Publishing, concealing, helping, threatening, departing, and returning should
  have remembered consequences, with information spreading through plausible paths.

A reference-integrity check alone does not establish narrative consistency.
We must also check meanings, motives, causal connections, and contradictory claims.

## Pacing and player direction

The Director should notice which people, questions, risks, and mysteries interest
the crew and develop relevant opportunities. It should maintain multiple possible
leads without forcing the player to follow one prescribed story.

Allow quiet exploration, wonder, humor, friendship, ambiguity, and thoughtful
conversation. Constant emergencies and countdowns crowd out discovery. Urgency
should have a reason in the fiction and leave room for learning.

Local mysteries can reach satisfying answers while opening a broader perspective.
Avoid endlessly postponing every answer or recursively spawning identical
“follow-ups.” A genuine ending to one story is part of an endless expedition.

## Example of the intended experience

This is an illustration of the desired structure, not a mandatory authored quest.

The crew receives a transmission from an apparently empty region. Separating
interference reveals several overlapping speakers reciting names. Translation
recovers enough meaning to suggest a memorial, but the timing matches a nearby
stellar fluctuation.

The crew can ask a passing vessel about the names, triangulate the source, compare
the signal to the stellar readings, or respond directly. A local speaker explains
that their people preserve some memories through public transmission. Another
individual disputes who has the right to speak for the dead.

Investigating the source reveals an old installation whose maintenance history
complicates both accounts. The player's questions develop the culture and the
personal stakes. Their scans establish physical evidence. Neither source supplies
the entire truth automatically.

The crew might help preserve the installation, disclose a contested record,
negotiate access, investigate the stellar process, or leave. Each choice produces
different relationships and future leads. Returning later preserves the people,
facts, promises, and consequences established during the encounter.

## What would miss the point

- A collection of ship controls with little to discover by operating them.
- Signal processing that unlocks no meaningful message, insight, or response.
- Scans that return unexplained numbers and a completion percentage.
- “Conclude investigation” revealing an answer that the actions never uncovered.
- Writing a report merely to unlock the next system.
- Random names and paragraphs that have no relationship to each other or to play.
- Alien cultures reduced to one trait and a generic greeting.
- Dialogue that acknowledges everything but changes nothing.
- Repeated follow-up labels without new evidence, stakes, or choices.
- Beautiful prose that contradicts prior events or agrees with every player theory.

## The next playable milestone

Prioritize one compelling, dynamically generated discovery arc before expanding
the instrument catalogue or campaign systems. These are acceptance goals, not
claims about the current implementation:

- [ ] A player encounters an AI-generated phenomenon or civilization with a coherent
  underlying situation and a specific reason to become curious.
- [ ] A scan or decoded signal reveals a concrete clue before any conclusion button.
- [ ] The player can ask unanticipated follow-up questions and uncover connected,
  persistent cultural or historical detail.
- [ ] At least two discoveries change how the player understands the situation.
- [ ] Different investigative or diplomatic approaches produce different supported
  discoveries and consequences.
- [ ] The player receives a satisfying local answer or outcome and can choose a
  meaningful further lead, without needing to type a report to make the story move.
- [ ] Leaving, reloading, and returning preserve facts, relationships, unresolved
  questions, and the effects of the player's choices.
- [ ] A playtest using a live AI provider demonstrates these properties, including
  questions and approaches outside a scripted regression path.
- [ ] The player can explain what they learned and why they want to explore further.

Technical reliability remains necessary. The acceptance test for fun is curiosity,
discovery, agency, and a memorable unfolding story.
